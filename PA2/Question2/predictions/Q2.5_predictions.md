# Q2.5 — Predictions before Testing the Baseline

Written and committed **before** model A was trained and before any experiment in
"Testing the Baseline" (Tables 3–4, Figures 3–5) was run. Inputs used: Figure 1 and the reach estimate (Q2.3) below.

## Reach estimate used for the prediction (Q2.3)

- `pixel_unshuffle(4)`: one cell of the 16×16 grid = 4 original pixels. It moves nothing.
- Each ConvLSTM layer has one 3×3 convolution on `[u_τ, h_{τ−1}]`, which moves information by at most 1 cell = 4 px.
  The gate/cell updates are element-wise, so they do not move information.
- 1×1 output convolution and `pixel_shuffle`: 0.
- Longest path inside one step τ: `u_τ` (or `h¹_{τ−1}`) → conv of layer 1 → `h¹_τ` → conv of layer 2 → `h²_τ` → 1×1 → `x̂_{τ+1}`.
  That is 2 convolutions × 1 cell × 4 px = **8 px per time step** along a row or column
  (≈ 11.3 px along a diagonal, since a 3×3 kernel also reaches diagonal neighbours).
  The path from `h²_{τ−1}` passes through only one convolution: 4 px.

## Figure 1 facts used

- The direction histogram covers all directions. Every 20° bin holds 2.6–8.2% of kept pixels on 50 videos
  (4.0–6.9% on a 1,000-video supplementary check), against 5.6% for a uniform distribution.
- Median speed is 3.3 px/frame and the 95th percentile is 4.3 px/frame. So 2× is about 6.6 px/frame
  (95th percentile 8.6), and 3× is about 10 px/frame (95th percentile 13).

## Predictions

**Mirror test.** Model A will mostly pass on *motion*: mirrored trajectories use directions that are common
in training, and the convolutional motion rule is local. Mirrored digits, however, are new shapes. I expect E_M
to be somewhat higher than the original E(5) (roughly 10–30% higher), with the extra error in the later horizons as
shape distortion or blur rather than wrong motion. C_M will be clearly non-zero but well below E(5).

**Rotation test.** The same reasoning holds for the motion, but rotated digits look even less familiar than mirrored
ones. I expect the rotation test to be "passed less well" than the mirror test: E_R > E_M and C_R > C_M. The difference
should come from appearance, not from motion.

**Speed.** With an 8 px/step reach and 3.3 px/frame typical motion:
- 1×: comfortably inside reach.
- 2× (≈ 6.6 px/frame): still mostly inside the reach but near its edge, and faster than anything seen in training.
  I expect noticeable degradation: E(2) clearly higher than at 1×, but predictions still recognisably move the digits.
- 3× (≈ 10 px/frame, often above 8 px): beyond the one-step reach for most videos.
  **I predict model A breaks down at 3×**: digits will lag behind or stall near their last input position and blur or
  fade out. E(2) at 3× will approach or exceed Copy-last.
